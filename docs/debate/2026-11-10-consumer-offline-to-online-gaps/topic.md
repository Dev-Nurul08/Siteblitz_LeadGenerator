# Topic

The founder's words (translated from Hinglish, 2026-11-10):
"Zomato basically solves this: we need food anywhere, anytime and we can order it - and it gives access to
many things, home food and restaurant food. Same with Blinkit: not everything for daily life is on Blinkit
yet, but a lot is, and they deliver fast. OYO only decorated rooms and posted photos. Uber gives us a cab
or any vehicle on hire based on distance. Rapido made one major change: bike and scooter riders can do it
too, which helped college students a lot. I feel they mostly built an application, did marketing, and one
or two more things - they set up a whole management system and it works. That is type one.
Type two: something done regularly offline that has not come online yet. On Shark Tank India a Delhi boy
built an app where anyone books a scrap (bhangar) pickup, local scrap dealers who roam around have accounts,
and they come and collect it; many scrap dealers can join. Research it. A business that was totally offline
was converted to online - list what limitations and problems they faced. Mainly find problems that are
real in India and worth building a brand on. Outside India is fine to mention, but India is the main point.
No B2B: we have no business, and B2B already has Amazon, Alibaba, IndiaMART, and we have no field team.
I have no idea what consumer things exist. Think as if you both have more than 50 years of experience as
CEO / founder, and I am a total fresher - older in age, can do normal work, but no idea about this. Any term
like EBITDA or gross - calculate it yourselves and explain everything clearly."

Constraints now fixed by the founder: consumer only; no own field team at start (aggregating existing
independent providers is allowed); fresher founder; India first.

## Agenda
1. Mental model: correct the founder's belief that Zomato / Blinkit / OYO / Uber / Rapido "only built an app, did marketing and a management system". What did each really build (supply, density, standardisation, capital, unit economics) - with numbers and sources.
2. Offline-to-online case studies in India (ScrapUncle and at least 7 more, live and dead): what the offline activity was, how they moved it online, the problems they hit, and what saved or killed them.
3. Long list: at least 20 consumer (B2C) activities in India still done mostly offline, where independent supply already exists (so a platform can aggregate it without hiring its own field team) - challenge, cut or extend Claude's seed list below.
4. Scoring with round-1 rubric (7 dimensions, 1-5 each, 3 knockout gates) plus new gates: consumer only, no own field team at start, executable by a fresher, low starting capital.
5. Top 5 with a full worked money example in rupees per idea (order value, take rate, gross margin, contribution margin, CAC, LTV, payback, monthly burn, EBITDA), every number labelled as assumption or sourced.
6. First idea and its 30-day test: what a fresher founder does each week, the budget, and pass / fail numbers.
7. Graveyard (traps with reasons) and a plain-English glossary of every business term used, each with one rupee example.
